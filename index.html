The Plan

Day by Day

1
Day One
Fri, Sept 5
Dublin → Galway
Arrival & Drive West
Drive City

Land at 6am, clear customs, grab a proper airport breakfast roll and coffee — a rite of Irish arrival. Pick up the rental car and drive straight west to Galway (2.5 hours). Arrive by 9:30am, drop bags at the hotel, and start exploring. The Latin Quarter, Salthill Promenade, and the weekend market await.

Dublin Airport → Galway
~2.5 hours on the M6. Straight west through the midlands with the morning sun behind you.
Salthill Promenade
A 2km seafront walk along Galway Bay with views toward the Aran Islands and the Burren. The traditional "kick the wall" at the end is non-negotiable.
Latin Quarter & Spanish Arch
Medieval streets, excellent bookshops, artisan food stalls, and a genuinely vibrant independent scene. Tigh Neachtain pub for your first pint.
Evening: Traditional Music
Monroe's Tavern or Tí Joe Mac's — live sessions in authentically atmospheric surroundings.
🏨
Stay in Galway
The House Hotel in the Latin Quarter (ideal location) or The g Hotel for a more design-forward boutique stay.
2
Day Two
Sat, Sept 6
Connemara + Cliffs of Moher
Hike Drive Food

One of the finest days Ireland can offer. Drive north into Connemara for a morning mountain hike over bog and glacial lake, then sweep south to the most iconic cliffs in Europe for golden-hour views over the Atlantic.

Connemara National Park — Diamond Hill Loop
7km moderate loop. Big panoramic views over the Twelve Bens, blanket bog, and the Atlantic. Allow 2.5–3 hours. Free entry.
Drive south to County Clare (~1.5 hrs)
Through Galway and into the Burren landscape as you approach the coast.
Cliffs of Moher — Late Afternoon
214m above the Atlantic. Walk south toward Hag's Head for the most dramatic, least-crowded section. Arrive after 3pm for softer light.
🏨
Stay in Doolin
Doolin Inn — charming village, 15 minutes from the Cliffs. Dinner at Gus O'Connor's Pub: legendary seafood chowder and live traditional music.
3
Day Three
Sun, Sept 7
The Burren + Drive to Kerry
Hike Drive

Start the morning in one of Ireland's strangest, most beautiful landscapes — a vast limestone plateau scattered with ancient tombs and wildflowers found nowhere else on earth. Then make the scenic drive south into Kerry.

The Burren — Mullaghmore Loop
Eerie karst limestone, rare Arctic-Mediterranean wildflowers growing side by side, Iron Age forts. Deeply atmospheric. Poulnabrone Dolmen (5,500 years old) is a short detour.
Lunch: The Roadside Tavern, Lisdoonvarna
Excellent pub food, local seafood, great atmosphere in a characterful Clare village.
Drive to Killarney (~2.5 hrs)
Through Limerick and the Golden Vale. The landscape softens beautifully as you enter Kerry.
Evening: Ross Castle Walk
A 15th-century castle on the shores of Lough Leane — magical at dusk.
🏨
Stay in Killarney
The Brehon (spa, great restaurant) or Aghadoe Heights for sweeping lake views. Dinner at Quinlan's Seafood Bar.
4
Day Four
Mon, Sept 8
Killarney National Park + Kenmare
Hike Food

A morning in one of Ireland's most beautiful national parks, then lunch in Kenmare — a perfectly preserved market town often called Ireland's finest foodie destination.

Killarney National Park — Morning Hike
Ambitious: Purple Mountain (4–5 hrs, summit views). Scenic moderate: Old Kenmare Road (10km through mountain passes). Easier: Torc Waterfall & Mangerton Valley.
Kenmare — Afternoon
30 minutes south of Killarney. Beautiful market town at the head of the Kenmare River estuary, ringed by mountains. Wednesday market if timing aligns.
Lunch / Dinner: Mulcahy's Restaurant
One of the best restaurants in Kerry. Local produce elevated with real finesse.
🏨
Stay in Kenmare (or Killarney)
Consider Sheen Falls Lodge outside Kenmare for a special night — one of the great Irish country house hotels with a Michelin-starred restaurant.
5
Day Five
Tue, Sept 9
Dingle Peninsula + Return to Dublin
Drive Food Dublin

An early start for the Dingle Peninsula — one of the most extraordinary coastal drives in Europe — before returning the car and settling into Dublin ahead of the wedding weekend.

Slea Head Drive
A 50km coastal loop from Dingle town. Ancient clocháns (beehive huts from early Christian monks), views of the Blasket Islands, dramatic sea stacks. Allow 3–4 hours including stops.
Lunch in Dingle Town
Out of the Blue — seafood-only, no menu, just the morning's catch. One of Ireland's best lunches. Follow with Murphy's Ice Cream on Strand Street.
Drive to Dublin (~3.5–4 hrs)
Return the rental car. Check into wedding hotel. An easy dinner at Bastible or Clanbrassil House before the festivities begin.
🏨
Check into Wedding Hotel, Dublin
Car returned. Wedding events begin Thursday — Wednesday is a gentle recovery day to explore Dublin at leisure.
6–8
Days Six–Eight
Wed–Sat, 10–13
Dublin — The Wedding
Wedding Dublin Food

Three days of celebration in Dublin, with time between events to enjoy one of Europe's finest small capitals. Fly out Sunday the 13th at 1pm.

Glasnevin Cemetery & Museum
One of the most atmospheric and historically significant places in all of Ireland. Genuinely moving.
Whiskey Distillery Tours
Teeling or Roe & Co distilleries — guided tastings of modern Irish whiskey in beautifully converted buildings.
Bewley's Oriental Café, Grafton Street
Restored Art Deco café for a proper coffee and bun — a Dublin institution.
Fly out Sunday, Sept 13 at 1pm
Leave hotel by 10am. Dublin Airport is 30–40 minutes from the city centre.

For the Hikers

Four Walks Worth Lacing Up For

Connemara, Day 2
Diamond Hill Loop
🥾 7km ⏱ 2.5–3 hrs 📊 Moderate
The signature walk of Connemara National Park. Panoramic views over the Twelve Bens, Atlantic coast, and endless blanket bog. Well-marked from the visitor centre.
The Burren, Day 3
Mullaghmore Loop
🥾 7km ⏱ 2–3 hrs 📊 Easy–Moderate
Through the heart of the Burren's limestone karst. Otherworldly terrain, ancient forts, rare wildflowers, and extraordinary silence. Stop at Poulnabrone Dolmen nearby.
Killarney, Day 4
Old Kenmare Road
🥾 10km ⏱ 3–4 hrs 📊 Moderate
A beautiful historic route through the heart of the national park. Mountain passes, ancient oak woodland, the Lakes of Killarney below. One of Kerry's finest walks.
Killarney, Day 4 (Alt)
Purple Mountain
🥾 12km ⏱ 4–5 hrs 📊 Challenging
The more ambitious option. A summit hike above Killarney with exceptional 360° views over the lakes, MacGillycuddy's Reeks, and the Kerry coast on a clear day.
Before You Go

Practical Notes

🚗
Driving
Left side of the road. Kerry and Connemara roads are narrow — go slow and enjoy it. Book an automatic if anyone isn't comfortable with manual left-hand drive. Budget for tolls on the M6 west from Dublin.
🌦
September Weather
58–65°F. Expect some rain, possibly brilliant sunshine — often both in the same hour. Waterproof layers essential for hikes. September is one of the better months: crowds thin, light is beautiful.
📅
Book in Advance
Aniar (Galway), Out of the Blue (Dingle), Sheen Falls Lodge, and Mulcahy's (Kenmare) all fill up weeks ahead in September. Book as soon as the itinerary is confirmed.
🍺
Pubs
Irish pubs are welcoming for adults through the evening — no pressure to rush. Traditional music sessions often start around 9pm and run late. Doolin in particular is exceptional for this.
💶
Currency & Payment
Euro. Cards accepted almost everywhere, but carry some cash for smaller pubs and market stalls. ATMs readily available in all towns.
📍
Getting Around
Rental car for Days 1–5 (return in Dublin on Day 5). Dublin city is very walkable; taxis and Ubers work well for the wedding weekend. No need for a car once you're back in the city.
